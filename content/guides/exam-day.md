Each course ends with two tests: a written test and a practical handling test. You need 80% on each one.

## The written test

- Usually 50 questions, mostly multiple choice. Some providers include true or false questions.
- Questions come from the course and the student handbook.
- Main areas: ACTS and PROVE, ammunition, action types, safe handling, storage, display and transport, and the owner's responsibilities.

## The practical test

- One on one with the instructor, using deactivated firearms and dummy ammunition. There is no live fire.
- You handle several firearms with different action types.
- You are asked to prove each firearm safe, load and unload it, and show safe handling throughout.
- Marking is continuous. Muzzle direction and trigger finger are watched the whole time, including while you talk.

## If you do not pass

- A part you passed normally stays passed. You retake only the part you failed.
- Retest timing and fees are set by the course provider. Ask your instructor.

## Check with your instructor

The exact format varies a little by province and provider. Your instructor's description of the day is the one to trust.
