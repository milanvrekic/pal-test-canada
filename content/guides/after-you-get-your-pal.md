Your licence card has arrived. Here is what most new licence holders do next, in the order it usually comes up.

## 1. Buying your first firearm

- Bring your licence. The seller has to confirm it's valid with the Canadian Firearms Program before a non-restricted firearm changes hands. They request a reference number, and the sale can only go ahead once it's issued.
- The reference number is good for 90 days, so a private sale can be arranged ahead of time.
- Restricted firearms go through a separate transfer that the Chief Firearms Officer approves, and they're registered to you. Since October 21, 2022, a national freeze means most individuals can't acquire a handgun. You can still take a restricted course and get an RPAL for other restricted firearms.
- Non-restricted firearms aren't registered, except in Quebec.

## 2. Store and transport it the legal way

The storage and transport rules apply from the first day you own a firearm, and they differ for non-restricted and restricted firearms. Check your situation with the [storage and transport rules checker](/rules).

## 3. Find a range

- A range is where you build the handling habits from your course. Many clubs have a new-member orientation and range safety rules.
- To take a restricted firearm to a range, it has to be an approved club or range, and you need an Authorization to Transport. Call the Canadian Firearms Program at 1-800-731-4000 to check whether a club is approved.
- Shootable, from the same team as this app, will show where you can shoot today and with which firearm, for BC and Alberta. It's launching soon.

## 4. Hunting: a separate provincial licence

A PAL lets you own firearms. It doesn't let you hunt. Every province has its own hunter education and hunting licence.

- In BC that means CORE hunter education, a free Fish and Wildlife ID (FWID), a hunting licence and, for big game, species licences. Our [BC hunting licence guide](/guides/how-to-get-a-hunting-licence-in-bc) walks through each step.
- Ducks and geese also need a federal Migratory Game Bird Hunting Permit.
- Seasons and closures change by zone and by species. Huntable shows what's open where you are in BC and Nova Scotia.
- Camping on a hunting trip? Campable shows BC recreation sites and whether campfires are allowed.

## 5. Fishing uses the same ID

In BC, the Fish and Wildlife ID you set up for hunting also buys your freshwater fishing licence. Both are sold in the province's WILD system, or in person at Service BC, FrontCounter BC or a vendor. Tidal (salt) waters need a separate federal licence. Fishable shows the freshwater rules for a lake or river.

## 6. Keep your licence valid

- A PAL is valid for five years. It expires five years after your first birthday after it was issued.
- The Canadian Firearms Program sends a renewal notice three months before it expires. Keep your mailing address up to date so you get it.
- Renew before the expiry date. If you own firearms, you need a valid licence to keep possessing them.
- Moving or changing your name? You have 30 days to tell the Canadian Firearms Program. It's a condition of your licence.
