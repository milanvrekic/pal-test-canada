Most people who fail do not fail on knowledge. They fail the practical on a habit they did not know they had, or the written test on storage and transport rules they tried to reason out instead of learning.

## On the practical test

### Losing track of the muzzle
This is the fault that costs the most. It usually happens when you stop thinking about the firearm: while you turn to answer the instructor, reach for ammunition, or set the firearm down.

**Fix:** decide where the safe direction is before you touch the firearm, and keep the muzzle there while you talk and move. Practise turning your body, not the firearm.

### Finger inside the trigger guard
Hands go to the trigger without being told to, especially when picking a firearm up or working the action.

**Fix:** give the finger a home. Lay it straight along the frame or stock every time you pick a firearm up, until it happens without thought.

### Skipping a PROVE step
Under nerves, people rush. The step dropped most often is the last one: examining the bore.

**Fix:** say each step out loud as you do it. "Point. Remove. Observe. Verify. Examine." Speaking slows you down and makes a missed step obvious.

### Not proving a firearm safe again
You proved it safe, put it down, and picked it back up without checking. A firearm that has left your hands is treated as unknown.

**Fix:** every pick-up starts with ACTS and PROVE. No exceptions, even when you set it down ten seconds ago.

### Getting the unloading order wrong
On firearms with a detachable magazine, working the action before removing the magazine loads another cartridge.

**Fix:** magazine out first, then open the action, then check the chamber.

### Closing the action when handing a firearm over
A firearm is passed with the action open so both people can see it is empty.

**Fix:** open, prove, pass. The person receiving it proves it safe too.

## On the written test

### Storage, display and transport
These questions depend on exact rules, and the rules differ between non-restricted and restricted firearms. Guessing from common sense gets them wrong.

**Fix:** learn the rules as two short lists, one per class, and practise them until you can say what changes between the two.

### Reading too fast
Words like "always", "never" and "only" decide many answers.

**Fix:** read the whole question and every option before choosing. There is enough time.

### Classification details
Barrel lengths, overall lengths and magazine limits are easy to mix up.

**Fix:** drill the numbers on their own until they stick.

## Before course day

- Know ACTS and PROVE by heart before you arrive. Then the course is practice, not first exposure.
- Sleep. It is a long day that ends with two tests.
- Ask for extra handling time if anything feels awkward. Instructors want you to pass safely.

This app is a study aid. It does not replace the instructor-led course, which is mandatory.
