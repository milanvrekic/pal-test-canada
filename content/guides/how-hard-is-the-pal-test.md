Most people who prepare pass. The material isn't advanced, but the pass mark is high and the practical test punishes habits you may not know you have.

## The pass mark is 80%, twice

Each course has a written test and a practical test, and you need 80% on each. On a 50-question written test that means no more than 10 wrong.

## The written test is about details

Questions come from the student handbook. The ones that catch people are exact rules: storage, display and transport, the differences between non-restricted and restricted firearms, and words like "always", "never" and "only" in the question. Guessing from common sense gets these wrong.

## The practical test is about habits

You handle deactivated firearms with dummy ammunition, and you are watched the whole time. The marks are lost on things like letting the muzzle drift while you talk, putting a finger in the trigger guard as you pick a firearm up, or skipping a step of PROVE under nerves. The guide "What fails people" lists them all and how to fix each one.

## How to know you are ready

- Score 90% or better on three practice exams in a row, with no topic under 80%. This app tracks that for you and turns the readiness card green.
- Say ACTS and PROVE out loud, from memory, while you handle something firearm-shaped at home.
- Work through the Spot the mistake drill until you find every mistake first time.

## If you don't pass

A part you passed normally stays passed, so you retake only the part you failed. Retest timing and fees are set by your course provider.

Sources: student handbook, 2014; this app's guides "What to expect on test day" and "What fails people", reviewed by a certified instructor.
