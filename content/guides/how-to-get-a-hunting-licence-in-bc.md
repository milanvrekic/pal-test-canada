To hunt in British Columbia you need CORE hunter education, a Fish and Wildlife ID (FWID), a hunting licence and, for big game, species licences. Your PAL is a separate federal licence for owning firearms.

## 1. Pass CORE

- The Conservation and Outdoor Recreation Education (CORE) program is BC's hunter education course. The BC Wildlife Federation runs it for the province.
- It covers safe firearms handling, conservation, ethics, the law, first aid and survival, and wildlife identification.
- You can study in a class or online, then finish with an exam in person with a CORE examiner.
- Passing adds hunting credentials to your FWID profile.

## 2. Get your Fish and Wildlife ID

- You need an FWID to buy any hunting licence or apply for a limited entry draw. It's free.
- Register online in WILD, the province's licensing system. Verifying a new account can take up to 3 days.
- Or register in person at a Service BC or FrontCounter BC office, or a licence vendor.

## 3. Buy your licences

- **Hunting licence:** needed to hunt anything in BC, and to buy species licences. Buy it online in WILD, or at Service BC, FrontCounter BC or a vendor.
- **Species licences:** needed for big game. They are printed on waterproof paper and you must carry them while hunting. Bought online, they come by mail, so buy in person if you plan to hunt within a few days.
- Species licences expire on March 31 of the licence year.
- **Migratory birds** (ducks, geese) also need a federal Migratory Game Bird Hunting Permit.

## 4. Limited entry hunting

Some hunts, such as certain big-game seasons, are by draw only. Apply for Limited Entry Hunting (LEH) draws through WILD before the draw closes.

## Trying it first: the initiation licence

- First-time hunters 18 and over can buy a one-time initiation hunting licence without doing CORE.
- You hunt with a licensed supervising hunter, and anything you take counts toward their bag limit.
- Initiation licence holders can't enter limited entry draws.

Seasons and closures change by zone. Huntable shows what's open where you are in BC, and in Nova Scotia.
