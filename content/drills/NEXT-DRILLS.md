## lean-truck (CFSC, p. 59)
Prompt: A gravel road at the edge of a forest. A hunter in a blaze orange vest and cap stands beside the open tailgate of a
pickup truck, pouring coffee from a thermos with both hands. His scoped bolt-action rifle is propped upright, leaning
against the side of the truck bed by itself, butt on the gravel, muzzle pointing up. Nobody is holding the rifle.
- Question: What is wrong here?
- Answer: His rifle is leaning against the truck on its own
- Wrong options: He is wearing a blaze orange vest and cap · The rifle's muzzle is pointed up, away from him · He has stopped at the edge of the forest
- Explanation: Never lean a loaded firearm against a vehicle, tree or wall: it could fall over and fire. Unload it and case it, or keep it in your hands.
- Source: 4.2.2. Load a firearm only for actual use, p. 59

## water-target (CFSC, p. 61)
Prompt: A calm lake shore. A shooter in a cap, shooting glasses and ear muffs kneels on the bank aiming a scoped
bolt-action rifle at a plastic bottle floating on the flat water about 30 metres out. His finger is straight along the
stock. No one else in the scene.
- Answer: He is aiming at a target floating on the water
- Wrong options: He is wearing shooting glasses and ear muffs · His finger is straight along the stock · He is steadying the rifle in a kneeling position
- Explanation: Never shoot if your bullet may hit water or a hard surface: the bullet or fragments can ricochet in unsafe directions.
- Source: 4.2.4. Be sure of your target and beyond, p. 61

## partner-muzzle (CFSC, p. 160)
Prompt: Two hunters in blaze orange walk single file along a narrow forest trail. The hunter at the back carries his
rifle in both hands at waist height with the muzzle pointing forward, straight at the back of the hunter in front.
The front hunter carries his rifle in a two-hand carry pointed off to the side, away from everyone.
- Answer: The rear hunter's muzzle is pointed at the hunter in front
- Wrong options: Both hunters are wearing blaze orange · The front hunter's muzzle points off to the side · They are walking in single file on the trail
- Explanation: Never use a carry that points the muzzle at another person. Choose your carry for where your companions are and the terrain.
- Source: 7.8. Carrying positions, p. 160

## hilltop (CFSC, p. 153)
Prompt: Open rolling hills in late autumn. A hunter in blaze orange lies prone on the grass, aiming a scoped rifle uphill
at a mule deer standing on the very crest of a ridge, silhouetted against the sky with nothing visible beyond it.
- Answer: The deer is on the crest of the hill, with no backstop behind it
- Wrong options: He is shooting from the steady prone position · He is wearing blaze orange · He has positively identified the animal as a deer
- Explanation: Don't shoot at game near the top of a hill. People or livestock may be in the line of fire over the hill. Be sure of your target and beyond.
- Source: 7.6. Safe handling of firearms outdoors, p. 153
